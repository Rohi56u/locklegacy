1 Executive Summary
“Aaj hum ek aisa product define karne wale hain jo kisi family ko emergency ya death ke baad apne loved
one ki financial aur digital life discover karne ke liye mahino tak struggle nahi karne dega. Hum funds ko
illegally transfer nahi karenge, passwords ko unsafe way mein share nahi karenge, aur AI ko legal authority
nahi denge.”
— Founder, opening line to the team
Legacy Lock is a secure continuity and claim-preparation platform. It organizes a person’s financial and
digital footprint into an encrypted vault, periodically confirms that person is still active, and – only when they
stop responding and death is independently corroborated – guides an authorized nominee through a verified,
institution-by-institution claim process.
It is deliberately not an automatic asset-transfer engine. The team’s core design principle is that discovery
and claim coordination is the real problem, not moving money – because no third party can legally bypass a
bank’s, insurer’s, or exchange’s own KYC and legal-heir process.
2 The Problem
2.1 A Real Scenario
Consider Arjun. His death is sudden. His family knows he likely had bank accounts, mutual funds,
insurance, crypto, and cloud documents – but not which institutions, which account identifiers, whether
nominees were even registered, or what the claim process requires. Every institution has its own KYC,
documentation, and legal-heir rules; there is no universal portal that can move money on their behalf.
2.2 Problem Statement & Product Promise

not an automatic beneficial owner.
5 Vault: Registration to Secure Storage
5.1 User Journey
1. Account creation – email/passkey sign-up with MFA; vault stays locked until passphrase setup is complete.
2. Vault passphrase – the user is warned this is critical. Keys are ideally derived on-device so the server never
sees a plaintext passphrase. A recovery kit is generated for offline safekeeping.
3. Guided onboarding checklist – add a first asset, add a trusted person, set a check-in schedule, write
emergency instructions, test recovery.
4. Asset inventory – categorized entries across bank/deposits, investments, insurance, crypto, cloud accounts,
documents, loans, subscriptions, and personal instructions.
MVP boundary. Raw internet-banking passwords, OTPs, crypto private keys, and seed phrases are not
collected. Instead the vault stores safer references: provider name, account hint, public wallet address,
recovery arrangement, support URL, and a professional contact.

6 Presence & Safety: Check-in and Escalation
This system is a continuity alert mechanism, not a death detector – the distinction the whole team needs to
hold onto.
6.1 Normal Flow
User sets a 30/60/90-day interval →system calculates the next check-in date →reminder sent by email/app/SMS
→user confirms “I’m active” →timestamp and channel logged →next date calculated.

Idempotency matters. Every transition needs an event ID, actor, timestamp, notification, retry behav-
ior, audit event, and rollback path. If the same job runs twice, it must never send a duplicate reminder,
open a duplicate claim case, or trigger a duplicate release.
7 Claim Workspace: Nominee & Claim Flow
Invitation
Identity Verification
Death Certificate
Claim Dashboard
AI Dossier
Controlled Release
• Invitation: contains no assets, balances, or sensitive data – only notice that a continuity case has started.
• Identity verification: name, government ID, relationship, and required documents; encrypted upload with
OCR-assisted extraction, each field shown with a confidence level (e.g. “Name extracted with medium confi-
dence – confirm or correct”).
• Death certificate: uploading starts a review – it does not trigger automatic release. The file is scanned,
encrypted, OCR’d, and queued for manual review.
• Claim dashboard: institution-wise cards (bank, mutual fund, insurance, crypto exchange, digital account)
each showing required documents, missing items, claim URL, contact, status, and next step.
• Dossier: AI places approved fields into a template – asset summary, masked identifiers, claimant info, doc-
ument checklist, institution contacts, instructions, source links, reviewer notes, and disclaimer. Labelled
“Institution-specific claim-preparation dossier” – never “guaranteed claim dossier”; each institution makes its
own final decision.
• Controlled release: the exact scope is shown before release, and high-risk items require two-person ap-
proval, re-authentication, an expiring link, a watermark, a download limit, and an audit log entry.
